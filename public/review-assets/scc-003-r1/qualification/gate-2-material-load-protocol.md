# SCC-003 qualification gate 2 — material and load protocol

Status: `PROTOCOL_READY / NOT_RUN`  
Identity: `SCC-003`  
Date: `2026-11-10`

## Question

Does one declared SCC-003 carrier produce a repeatable physical fold response, rather than only a valid display and local vertex assignment?

## Pre-registration boundary

Before cutting any specimen, select exactly one carrier: `G_18_23` or `G_20_23`. Do not mix their angular semantics in one run. Record material, thickness, sheet dimensions, crease process, tool radius, humidity, temperature, operator and specimen count. Blank fields invalidate the run.

## Controls

1. An uncreased blank from the same material batch.
2. A crease-assignment control with the mountain/valley assignment intentionally changed.
3. At least three independently prepared specimens for the selected carrier.

## Measurements

- sector angles and crease assignment before loading;
- force–displacement trace for every cycle;
- peak force and displacement at the declared capture state;
- residual displacement after unloading;
- self-contact, collision, tear, delamination and crease migration;
- cycle count at first irreversible change.

Units, instruments, calibration identifiers, sampling rate and acceptance thresholds must be entered before the run. No threshold may be chosen after viewing results.

## Procedure

1. Photograph and measure the flat specimen.
2. Apply the declared fold sequence at a fixed displacement rate.
3. Record loading and unloading without smoothing the raw trace.
4. Repeat the pre-registered cycle count.
5. Measure the unloaded specimen and record all stop events.
6. Export raw observations plus a signed run receipt; keep derived plots separate.

## Stop conditions

Stop on tear, delamination, tool slip, unplanned self-contact, sensor saturation or departure from the selected carrier. A stopped run is data, not a pass.

## Claim ceiling

This protocol can qualify a specimen and a loading procedure. Until executed, it establishes no material strength, self-support, resonance, biological folding, protein analogy or production readiness.
