# NEXAH Experience Review 03 — Radical Simplicity

## Review basis

This review evaluates the rendered first viewport and the public entrance as if
the visitor knows nothing about NEXAH or its internal architecture. It removes
presentation and choices; it does not remove rooms, authority boundaries,
content or deterministic capability.

## 1. Simplicity Review

The single idea of Home should be:

> I can begin here with a real question.

Before this review, the first viewport simultaneously asked the visitor to:

- choose how to enter NEXAH;
- understand “Orientation Space” and “Threshold”;
- type into a search- or chat-like field;
- distinguish explanations, Catalog lookup and registered routes;
- choose among four example questions;
- understand three product exclusions;
- choose among seven permanent navigation destinations.

The result was accurate but premature. It explained the instrument before the
visitor had decided to enter.

The revised Home begins with the visitor’s question. It contains no input,
examples, system terminology, catalog terminology or ORION language. The
primary action opens the existing Threshold, where the question properly
belongs. A single quiet alternative permits editorial exploration without a
question.

## 2. Elements removed

### Home

- the question input and submit form;
- four example-question pills;
- the internal canonical Observation-to-Calendar sentence;
- “Approved explanations · explicit catalog lookup · registered routes ·
  honest boundaries”;
- the three “not search / not chatbot / explicit paths” statements;
- six editorial-door cards;
- three guided-beginning rows;
- the complete-Catalog invitation;
- the supporting imports and now-unused Home styles.

The removed destinations still exist in Explore, Library and the global
navigation. Nothing was deleted from its owning room.

### Global frame

- **Begin** from desktop and mobile navigation, because Home now owns the
  beginning;
- the large shared footer manifesto and its three-line authority explanation.

The compact footer navigation remains, so long rooms still have an explicit
way out.

## 3. Elements postponed

- Explaining what NEXAH is waits until the visitor chooses **About** or asks the
  Threshold.
- Explaining the six editorial doors waits until **Explore**.
- Catalog volume and publication lookup wait until **Library**.
- ORION terminology waits until an existing registered request has crossed the
  Threshold.
- Architecture, provenance and evidence detail wait until inspection is
  requested.
- The internal canonical request waits behind **See one orientation** on
  Explore; it is no longer public Home copy.

Postponement here is not concealment. Each concept appears in the room that
owns it.

## 4. Navigation Review

### Keep

- Home
- Explore
- Laboratory
- Library
- Living Atlas
- About

These labels lead to stable rooms. Their current order moves from entrance and
discovery toward research, publications, relationships and purpose.

### Remove

- **Begin** as a permanent item.

Home already provides the beginning, and the NEXAH brand already returns to
Home. Keeping a second permanent entry made “Home” and “Begin” compete without
offering different responsibilities.

### Simplify later

“About” remains the least spatial label. **Why NEXAH** may better express its
purpose, but this review does not replace one label merely to create novelty.
That decision should be tested independently.

## 5. Hero Review

### Previous hero

**How would you like to enter NEXAH?** began with the system and asked a new
visitor to choose an entrance before they understood the invitation. The input
made the page resemble search or chat even though the surrounding copy denied
both.

### Revised hero

**What are you trying to understand?** begins with the visitor.

Supporting language is limited to:

> Bring one real question. NEXAH opens a place to begin.

The hero has one dominant action:

> Begin with a question

The quiet alternative, **Or explore without asking**, is deliberately less
prominent. It preserves freedom without creating a competing workflow.

No input appears on Home. The first interaction is editorial: choosing to
enter. The actual question is written only after that choice, inside the
Orientation Threshold.

## 6. Homepage Review

The revised Home is now one room rather than a compressed map of the entire
site. Its visual sequence is:

```text
Brand and quiet navigation
↓
A place to begin
↓
What are you trying to understand?
↓
Begin with a question
↓
Optional exploration
```

The page no longer teaches Library, Laboratory, Living Atlas or ORION. Those
rooms remain discoverable in the stable navigation and explain themselves upon
entry.

The registered deterministic journey remains discoverable through Explore as
**See one orientation — Follow an existing question until its visible path
reaches a boundary.** This exposes capability, not internal terminology. Its
explicit canonical request is preserved and cannot be overwritten by an
existing session question.

## 7. Recommendation

### Keep

- the visitor-first question;
- one dominant Threshold entrance;
- one quiet Explore alternative;
- the stable room navigation;
- the calm visual field and existing path motif;
- the registered journey behind a later, human-readable invitation.

### Simplify

- continue moving specialist language into the room that owns it;
- let Explore own entry options and Library own completeness;
- keep the first viewport free of examples, exclusions and architecture;
- revisit “About” only as a separate language decision.

### Remove

- duplicate beginnings;
- input controls on Home;
- internal examples before context exists;
- statements that explain what the product is not;
- global manifesto copy repeated after every room;
- permanent navigation actions that duplicate Home.

## Result

Home no longer asks the visitor to understand NEXAH before entering it. ORION
remains a room, not the house. The first screen now communicates one complete
invitation:

> You may begin with a real question.
